Maddy sat outside, just out of sight from the door. It was still light out. She could hear her aunt and uncle chatting happily inside.

They had gotten a letter from one of their sons and were just, so happy all day after the priest read it for them at the church.

Maddy was happy that Mariano was doing well, but. . . she was jealous. Why couldn’t they be her parents? She was there now, on Burano. In their house, but was it still really her home? It wasn’t really her parents and even though they were really nice, she felt it.

The February wind picked up the icy mist and prickled her cheek. She burried her face in her arms, glaring at the water.

“Madelena, are you out here? Come in! It’s cold!”

Far off in the water, Maddy saw something gleam in the water.

“A mermaid.” She decided. She stood up and dusted her hands on her dress. Turning, determined, she stomped inside.

“There you are!” Aunt Caterina handed her a bowl. Fish. Of course. Why was Aunt Caterina so nice when Maddy’s dad was so not nice?

“Aunt Cat.” Maddy declared.

Aunt Caterina looked up. “Mmm?”

“Did you know that my mom is a mermaid?”

Aunt Caterina smiled, turned and scratched her neck.

Maddy tucked her hair behind her ear, pulling on the long red-blonde strands. “Grandma, she was a mermaid.”

Aunt Caterina chuckled.

“But then, when Mom was born, she was a mermaid to, but she had legs and met Dad and then they had me.” Maddy’s eyes glazed over as she stared into her warm, gently steaming fish soup. “It was really sad when Mom had to go back into the water. It made Dad sad to, that’s why-”

“Maddy.” Aunt Cat had a funny look on her face. Sad, but angry but worried to? “That’s enough.”

Maddy pulled her lips in and pressed her thumb nail against them.

She sat and quietly ate her fish soup.

Uncle Marco came back in, flicking the cold off his fingertips.

Aunt Cat handed him a bowl of fish soup to.

“Well thank you dear.” He said and they talked more about Maddy’s cousins.

“I’m so happy Mariano and Baby Marco are doing so well. I’m eager for word from Pietro.”

“It’ll come dear. I don’t think we can call him ‘Baby Marco’ anymore. He’s a grown 17 year old man now!”

Maddy sighed and imagined what her life would have been like as a baby mermaid in the sea.
